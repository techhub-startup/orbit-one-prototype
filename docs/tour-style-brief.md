# Orbit One welcome tour: style brief

Purpose: a first-time viewer watches about 50 seconds and wants to try the app. Sell the feeling ("nothing slips, and nothing leaves your phone"), not the machinery.

## 1. Placards (the big headline on the stage, and the caption strip on phones)

1. **One idea per placard.** One beat gets one headline. If a second idea shows up, it gets its own beat. Apple's slide designers keep to one theme per slide, and a statistic that matters gets a slide of its own [1][2].
2. **Few words.** Headline: 1 to 3 words ("It notices.", "It learns."). Support line: 6 words or fewer. Apple keynote text slides average about three words [3][5].
3. **Large, confident type, lots of empty space.** No bullets, no labels, no sentences that need reading. The big headline stays the largest thing on the stage besides the phone. On the iPhone 17 Air slide, about 90% of the frame is empty [2][4].
4. **Show the product first, headline a beat later.** Each beat opens on the phone doing something. The headline fades in about 300 to 450 ms after the visual event, so the eye sees the event first and the words confirm it [2].
5. **Keep motion restrained.** Use one soft fade-and-rise for each headline. No bouncing, no build-ups, no staggered bullets. Animation should never cover for too much content [4].
6. **Use contrast.** Show "what is" (a notification you'd normally have to handle) against "what could be" (it's already handled) in every chapter [6][7].
7. **Give every placard time to be read.** A placard stays up for at least about 2 s. Short confirmation beats (a tap, a toast) keep the current placard, because the screen already shows the result. When only the support line changes, the headline stays put and only the support line fades in, so nothing blinks.

## 2. Voiceover (writing)

1. **Write for the ear.** Use short present-tense declaratives, contractions and active verbs. One idea per sentence, and if a sentence needs a comma to survive, split it [8][9].
2. **Second person, benefit first.** The viewer is the hero ("you pay it", "you carry enough"). Say what it does for you, not how it works: no "classifier", "model" or "pipeline" [10][11].
3. **Don't read the screen.** The voice adds meaning the picture can't carry, like why it matters or what happens next. It never repeats the placard word for word.
4. **Rule of three.** Group wins in threes, and keep closers short and rhythmic. The iPhone launch used "an iPod, a phone, an internet communicator". The "Privacy. That's iPhone." spot lists "Your location. Your messages. Your heart rate after a run." [7][12][13]
5. **End on something memorable.** Use one short emotional line, then the brand and tagline in silence ("Privacy. That's iPhone." / "Think different.") [12][14].
6. **Budget words.** Conversational VO runs about 150 to 160 wpm, and a 30 s spot holds about 75 to 80 words. Aim lower when pictures carry the story [9][11]. For this voice (Kokoro af_heart at 0.9, about 2.7 words/s), keep each sentence to 10 words or fewer, and each clip to two sentences at most (the three longest clips, agent-pick 11, pay-match 13 and fix-reply 12 words, are two short sentences each).

## 3. Voiceover (delivery and timing)

1. **Warm, unhurried, confident.** Use one consistent narrator (the existing Kokoro af_heart takes at speed 0.9). Don't speed up to fit a slot. Cut words instead [9].
2. **Start each line on its visual.** A line starts within about 0.3 s of the on-screen event it describes, never before the thing exists on screen.
3. **Pause after key beats.** Leave about 0.4 to 0.8 s after a line before the next one. Silence is allowed only while something is visibly moving (a tap, a sheet, a row sliding in). Never leave more than about 2.5 s without voice or motion [9].
4. **Let the ending land.** Closing line, then a short beat (about 0.6 s), then the logo and tagline. After that, hold the call to action with no voice.

## 4. Patterns used in this tour

- Event, then meaning: *[banner drops]* "Oh, a notification just came in." / *[agent panel]* "Orbit One picks it up instantly. Right here, on your phone."
- Split declaratives: "It's a delivery. Arriving by seven tonight." / "So it becomes a task. Right where it belongs."
- Contrast: "You pay it later, in CRED." (spelled "Cred" in the TTS text so it is spoken as a word) then "Orbit One sees the payment, and marks the bill paid."
- Rule of three on the placards: "It notices." / "It thinks." / "It understands." (The spoken recap "A bill, caught early. A delivery, turned into a task. A payment, spotted on its own." belonged to closing B, which was dropped because it re-told what had just been shown.)
- Closer: "You carry enough. Let Orbit One remember the rest." then *Orbit One. Your life, nothing slips.*
